1　海辺の駅

ホームに降りると、潮の匂いがした。三十歳の紗季は小さなスーツケースを置き、耳の銀の葉に触れた。五年前、律が選んだイヤリング。今日つけてきた理由を、まだうまく言葉にできなかった。 律から届いたのは、港の写真展の案内だった。「土曜日が最終日。よかったら」。東京で本の装丁をする紗季と、この町で写真館を継いだ律は、五年前まで恋人だった。離れて暮らすうち、会う約束がいつも「落ち着いたら」になった。別れを口にしたのは紗季だった。 駅の時計の下に律がいた。三十二歳になった彼は、昔と同じように片方の袖だけ折っている。 「迎え、頼んでないよ」 「うん。会場までの坂だけ案内したくて」 彼は荷物を奪わず、隣に並んだ。その距離に、紗季は少し安心した。 海沿いの道で、律が聞いた。 「明日は何時に帰る？」 「十一時。それまでは、ちゃんとここにいる」 言ってから、紗季自身が驚いた。仕事の通知を切り、携帯を鞄にしまった。

The smell of salt reached Saki as she stepped onto the platform. She set down her small suitcase and touched the silver leaf at her ear. Ritsu had chosen those earrings five years ago. At thirty, she still could not quite explain why she had worn them today. He had sent an invitation to his exhibition of harbor photographs. “Saturday is the last day. Come if you’d like.” Saki designed book covers in Tokyo; Ritsu had taken over his family’s photography studio in this town. They had been a couple until five years ago. Living apart, they kept postponing their visits until things settled down. Saki had been the one to end it. Ritsu was waiting beneath the station clock. Now thirty-two, he still rolled up only one sleeve. “I didn’t ask you to meet me.” “I know. I just wanted to show you the hill up to the exhibition.” He did not take her luggage from her. He walked beside her, and the space he left made her feel a little easier. On the coast road, he asked, “What time do you leave tomorrow?” “Eleven. Until then, I’m going to be here. Properly here.” Her own answer surprised her. She muted her work notifications and put her phone away.
